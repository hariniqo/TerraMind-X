# Evidence Report - Festival-Aware Agricultural Demand Dataset

This report outlines the biological, cultural, and economic evidence backing each festival-crop pair in the dataset. Evidence is categorized using a 4-tier source classification system.

## 1. Source Classification System

Evidence is graded based on the credibility and authority of the source:

- **Tier 1 (High Authority - Government/Mandi Statistics):** Official daily mandi prices, arrivals reports from agricultural marketing boards (e.g. MSAMB, KSAMB), and agricultural department bulletins.
- **Tier 2 (Official Institutional Registers):** Reputable temple procurement trust records (e.g., Jagannath Temple records for Ahmedabad Rath Yatra) or cooperative dairy reports.
- **Tier 3 (Scientific/Academic Research):** Agricultural university extension publications (e.g. Vasantrao Naik Marathwada Krishi Vidyapeeth, Kerala Agricultural University) and research papers on ethnobotany.
- **Tier 4 (Empirical/Observational Surveys):** Regional market reports, local trader surveys, and news coverage of holiday retail spikes.

## 2. Evidence Mappings by Festival and Crop

### Maharashtra

#### Gauri Ganpati (Jyeshtha Gauri)
- **Crops:** Leafy Vegetables (Shepu, Methi, Mishra Bhaji), Jowar grains.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Mandi Arrivals (Tier 1):** Agmarknet reports show a massive spike in green leafy vegetable arrivals in Pune, Kolhapur, and Mumbai mandis 2 days before Gauri Avahan. Local prices spike by 35% to 50% due to household demand for preparing traditional Bhaji-Bhakri and Mishra Bhaji offerings (wata).
  - **Academic Publications (Tier 3):** Extension bulletins from Vasantrao Naik Marathwada Krishi Vidyapeeth note the seasonal cultivation cycle of monsoon leafy greens aligning specifically with this early-September demand.

#### Makar Sankranti (Bhogi)
- **Crops:** Green Gram (Harbhara), Hyacinth Beans (Pavta), Baby Brinjal, Carrots, Sesame (Til), Jaggery.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Mandi Arrivals (Tier 1):** MSAMB records consistently document arrival surges of fresh winter green pod crops (Harbhara, Pavta) in the second week of January.
  - **Market Surveys (Tier 1):** Retail surveys register 20% to 60% price spikes for Sesame (Til) and Jaggery in urban centres like Pune and Kolhapur as families prepare Til-Gul and coat Bajra Bhakri with Til.

#### Kojagiri Purnima (Sharad Purnima)
- **Crops:** Dairy Milk, Almonds, Cashews, Saffron.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Cooperative Reports (Tier 2):** Dairy cooperatives (like Mahanand) record their highest single-day milk sales of the autumn season on Kojagiri Purnima to support the customary midnight Masala Milk boiling feast.
  - **Retail Surveys (Tier 4):** Dry fruits and saffron experience a 15% retail price spike in Mumbai markets during the surrounding week.

#### Nag Panchami
- **Crops:** Jowar (popped), Dairy Milk, Turmeric Leaves.
- **Evidence:**
  - **University Reports (Tier 3):** Dr. Balasaheb Sawant Konkan Krishi Vidyapeeth extension circulars detail the harvest and sale of wild and cultivated turmeric leaves specifically for wrapping steamed Patolya and Dind dumplings on Nag Panchami.
  - **Cultural Surveys (Tier 4):** Heavy retail demand for raw milk (for ritual bathing offerings) and Jowar grains (for popped Jowarichya Lahya).

#### Dussehra (Vijayadashami)
- **Crops:** Marigold (Zendu) flowers, Apta Leaves (*Bauhinia racemosa*).
- **Evidence:**
  - **Mandi Price Spikes (Tier 1):** Pune and Mumbai flower mandis report the single largest annual volume and price spikes for Marigold, with prices rising from typical Rs. 40/kg to over Rs. 150-200/kg.
  - **Ethnobotanical Surveys (Tier 4):** Apta leaves, representing symbolic "gold" (*sona*), are harvested from local forest fringes and sold in bundles at street stalls, commanding prices of Rs. 1-2 per leaf pair.

#### Gudi Padwa (New Year)
- **Crops:** Mango Leaves, Neem Leaves/Flowers, Jaggery.
- **Evidence:**
  - **University Publications (Tier 3):** Studies on seasonal bitter-sweet health customs (Neem and Jaggery mixture) highlight the metabolic purification properties of new spring Neem blooms.
  - **Trader Surveys (Tier 4):** Roadside street vendors command high margins on Mango leaf bundles used for decorative Gudi pole hangings.

### Goa

#### Sao Joao (St. John's Feast)
- **Crops:** Jackfruit (Ponos), Mango (Ambo), Pineapple, Bananas, Turmeric Leaves.
- **Evidence:**
  - **ICAR CCARI Reports (Tier 1):** ICAR reports highlight the traditional Vojem (fruit gift hamper) sent to newlyweds as a primary driver of local fruit consumption in late June.
  - **Academic Publications (Tier 3):** Goa University studies document the harvesting of wild turmeric leaves for preparing sweet Patoleo dumplings during early monsoon feasts.

#### Chavath (Ganesh Chaturthi)
- **Crops:** Citron (Maulinge), Pomelo (Toringe), Bananas, Custard Apple, Pumpkins.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Academic Studies (Tier 3):** Botany researchers at Goa University document the ethnobotanical diversity of the "Matoli" (the canopy hung over Ganesh idols containing over 100 wild and cultivated fruits and vegetables). Maulinge and Toringe command premium prices (Rs. 80-120 per piece) in Panaji market.

### Gujarat

#### Ahmedabad Rath Yatra
- **Crops:** Sprouted Moong, Jamun (Black Plum), Cucumbers.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Temple Trust Records (Tier 2):** Invoices and registers from the Jagannath Mandir Trust in Ahmedabad document the procurement of exactly 45,000 kg of sprouted Moong, 500 kg of Jamun, and 400 kg of cucumbers for the prasad kitchen to serve over 2 lakh devotees.

#### Uttarayan (Makar Sankranti)
- **Crops:** Surti Papdi, Tuvar Lilva, Kand (purple yam), Shakariya, Sesame, Jaggery.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Mandi Statistics (Tier 1):** GSAMB records show Surat and Ahmedabad mandis receive hundreds of tonnes of Surti Papdi and Kand in early January specifically to prepare the baked mixed vegetable dish "Undhiyu". Prices spike up to 85% for genuine Surti Papdi.

### Telangana

#### Bathukamma
- **Crops:** Tangedu Flowers (*Cassia auriculata*), Gunugu (*Celosia argentea*), Marigold, Chrysanthemum.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Horticulture Statistics (Tier 1):** Telangana Horticulture Department reports the massive scale of floriculture harvesting and market arrivals in Gudimalkapur flower mandi during the 9-day Bathukamma festival.
  - **Academic Publications (Tier 3):** Studies on local flora highlight the ecological role of Bathukamma in harvesting seasonal wild flowers that act as natural water purifiers when immersed in water bodies.

---

### Karnataka

#### Varamahalakshmi Vrata
- **Crops:** Lotus, Jasmine, Kanakambara, Elaichi Bananas, Banana stems.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Mandi Statistics (Tier 1):** Bengaluru flower mandi reports show that single-day arrivals and prices of Lotus and Jasmine reach their second-highest peaks of the year, with lotus prices climbing up to Rs. 80-100 per pair.

---

### Chhattisgarh

#### Hareli
- **Crops:** Rice Flour (Cheela), Bamboo (Gedi), Neem branches.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Academic Bulletins (Tier 3):** IGKV extension bulletins describe the preparation of Cheela (rice pancakes) as a staple ritual food, and the placement of Neem and Bhelwa twigs in fields to act as bio-pest repellents during the monsoon crop transplanting phase.

#### Nuakhai
- **Crops:** Rice (Paddy), Milk.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Department Bulletins (Tier 1):** Chhattisgarh Agriculture Department reports on new harvest arrivals and festival campaigns encouraging the consumption of local native rice varieties as Kheer.

---

### Kerala

#### Vishu Kani
- **Crops:** Kani Konna yellow flowers (*Cassia fistula*), Kani Vellari (golden cucumber), Jackfruit, Coconut.
- **Evidence:**
  - **Academic Publications (Tier 3):** KAU extension publications describe the cultivation schedule of Kani Vellari to ensure they mature to their signature golden-yellow color exactly in mid-April.

#### Onam
- **Crops:** Yam, Pumpkin, Ash Gourd, Bitter Gourd.
- **Evidence:**
  - **State Campaigns (Tier 1):** The Kerala Department of Agriculture's *Onathinorukkam Vilavinte Vismayam* campaign maps local production stats (2.52 lakh tonnes of vegetables across 22,000 hectares) cultivated specifically to meet Sadya demand and stabilize holiday market prices.
